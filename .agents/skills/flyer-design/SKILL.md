# Flyer design

Design a composed graphic people notice, understand, and act on, not a miniature landing page. Be collaborative: make a reasoned proposal, keep decisions editable, and refine from feedback.

## Scope the work

Adapt the workflow to the request. For a new design or substantial redesign, explore distinct concepts and develop an editable master. For a critique, inspect the supplied work and give prioritized findings; create revised artifacts only when requested. For a focused revision, preserve the existing direction and source, change the requested elements, and inspect affected exports without restarting concept exploration.

Read applicable brand guidelines as the authority for identity restrictions and approved exceptions. Use layout recipes as examples unless explicitly required. Choose photography, typography, illustration, or collage on merit; authentic photography is not a mandatory lead. Match the energy to the subject rather than assuming every piece should be safe or edgy.

Use the available image-generation skill for generated bitmap art and the PDF skill when producing or inspecting print PDFs. Tool limitations must be disclosed rather than hidden behind claims of editable source or visual inspection.

## 1. Establish the brief and verified facts

Record the audience, event, desired action, viewing context, formats, and constraints. Read supplied files; never claim access from filenames alone. Preserve an untouched original for revisions.

Make a fact sheet with sources for title, date/year, time and zone where relevant, venue/address, cost, eligibility, registration, organizer and sponsor roles. Check weekday/date agreement. Separate verified facts, proposed wording, and missing information. Never invent event details, raffle terms, prices, URLs, schedules, attendance claims, or sponsors; do not promote an email suggestion into confirmed copy. Flag contradictions rather than silently resolving them.

Ask only questions that block meaningful progress. Otherwise proceed with a labeled draft, keeping missing facts out of production exports. Publication requires resolved essentials. Do not publish or send anything without authorization.

## 2. Inspect brand assets and references

Read available guidelines, website styles, repository assets, font files/licenses, approved logo variants, and original photos. Record actual paths or URLs. Prefer authoritative tokens to sampled approximations. Label inferred fonts and substitutions.

Create a compact token sheet:
- Palette: base, ink, brand color, optional accent; hex values and roles.
- Type: family, available weights, headline/detail roles, availability/license.
- Geometry: grid, alignment, margins, spacing rhythm, image treatment.
- Identity: logo variants, clear space, sponsor hierarchy.

Study a small set of relevant flyers. Record concrete observations: type scale, photo area/crop, whitespace, alignment, palette and recurring devices. Distinguish what you saw, your interpretation, and your recommendation. If a reference is inaccessible, say so; never invent visual findings. Borrow compositional principles, not another organization's identity, signature artwork, or unlicensed images.

Brand fidelity does not mean copying website navigation or cards. A brand font is not disqualified because a generic design prompt dislikes it.

## 3. Set the reading order

Write the hierarchy in one line, usually event identity → date/time and place → reason to attend → action/organizer. Adapt to the brief: an unfamiliar venue or urgent deadline may deserve greater weight. Sponsors and decoration must not overpower the event.

Reduce copy before reducing type size. Group related facts, remove redundant labels, and retain practical attendance information. Move secondary information to a caption or registration page only if the flyer still works when shared alone.

Choose one main visual idea: distinctive type, authentic photography, purposeful illustration, or coherent collage. Explain its connection to the event in one sentence; keep supporting elements quieter.

## 4. Render 2–3 distinct thumbnail concepts

Use real copy and representative assets to create small visual compositions, not just written descriptions. Vary structure, not just accent colors. Possible directions:
- Photo-led: strong crop and a clear type field.
- Type-led: title as the main image, disciplined details.
- Graphic/illustration-led: an event-specific visual language.

These are possibilities, not required templates. Compare focal point, readability, brand fit, emotional tone and crop feasibility side by side. Recommend one with a brief rationale. Show a contact sheet when the user wants selection; otherwise choose provisionally. Do not polish three complete finals or endlessly generate decorative variants.

## 5. Refine the selected composition

### Type
Use one family or a purposeful pairing, usually no more than two. Define title, essential-detail, support and credit roles with deliberate scale, weight, width, leading and tracking. Prefer scale contrast to many effects. Inspect optical alignment, line breaks, awkward single words and missing glyphs. Do not stretch or compress letterforms. Capitals and italics are tools, not universal bans; keep practical details easy to read.

### Layout and color
Use a deliberate grid and repeatable spacing. Align related edges and use space before adding boxes or rules. White space is not an invitation to add stickers. Start with brand colors and clear light/dark separation; assign accents specific jobs. Gradients and textures are allowed when they serve the concept, not as automatic polish.

### Imagery and crop
Prefer relevant authentic imagery when it communicates the actual event/community. Check usable resolution. Do not present stock or generated imagery as real participants or venues.

Preview crop alternatives. Identify focal people/action, then reduce distracting unrelated uniforms, race bibs, signage or background clutter when requested. Keep faces and meaningful gestures intact; avoid awkward limb cuts or accidental emphasis on competing brands. Never alter clothing, identities or logos to disguise affiliation. If a crop cannot work, choose another approved image or composition. Keep the original and record crop coordinates or a nondestructive mask. Recheck crops for each ratio.

Place essential type over a genuinely quiet image area or a controlled contrast field, never across faces or busy texture.

### Identity and decoration
Use approved logos without redrawing, distorting, arbitrary recoloring or cropping, except for treatments explicitly permitted by the identity guidelines, such as an approved underline edge-bleed. Select the correct light/dark variant, preserve clear space, and inspect small lettering and background contrast. Size logos according to their role.

Festive illustration can be excellent when intentional and stylistically coherent. A stock hat, snowfall, glow or gradient added after the hierarchy is settled must earn its place. If removal strengthens the message, remove it. Repeated decoration-resizing requests may indicate a weak concept: reconsider its role rather than continuing a decoration loop.

## 6. Build an editable master and format adaptations

Use a suitable vector, layered-layout or source-based document. Keep text editable, crops nondestructive, layers/components named, and assets linked or embedded reliably. Typeset final event copy deterministically; do not rely on generated lettering for accurate copy or logos. Generated illustration, when useful, should be composited with editable real typography and approved identity assets.

Confirm destination specs. Common starting points, not fixed requirements: 1080×1350 feed portrait, 1080×1920 story, and a specified physical print size. Recompose across ratios rather than stretching or blindly center-cropping. Verify current platform overlay-safe areas for the actual destination.

Export social files in sRGB at requested dimensions. For print, confirm trim, printer bleed and safe margins, image resolution at placed size (often around 300 ppi), color profile, embedded fonts and PDF standard. A screen PDF is not automatically press-ready. Add bleed/crop marks only per printer specifications; disclose unknown print requirements. Encode only verified QR destinations, test exported codes, and include a readable URL when useful.

## 7. Inspect actual exported files

Open rendered exports, not only source or specifications. Check:
1. Thumbnail: one focal point, coherent reading order, distinct concept.
2. Phone size, roughly 360–430 CSS pixels wide, no zoom: immediately identify the event, when and where, then action. Enlarge essential details if needed.
3. Full size: faces/crops, logo lettering, image quality, line breaks, clipping, alignment, missing glyphs and artifacts.
4. Intended print size where applicable: small copy, margins, resolution.

Use contrast measurements as diagnostics: aim for 4.5:1 for small essential text and 3:1 for large text against the actual background, then inspect visually. These are useful accessibility targets, not proof a static flyer is fully accessible. Provide plain-text event copy or suggested alt text for digital distribution.

Read every visible fact against the fact sheet and ensure each format retains essentials. Test links and QR codes without submitting forms. Report checks you could not perform. Never claim visual verification without opening the renders.

Do a focused critique: what competes with the title, what is too small, what is redundant, and what can be removed? Refine, re-export, and re-inspect. Prefer one or two purposeful refinement rounds to endless additions; honor further feedback and keep checkpoints. Persistent structural problems require a composition change, not another sticker.

## 8. Handoff and iteration

Deliver selected social export(s), print PDF when requested, editable source, required assets or an asset manifest, and brief reproduction/export instructions. Include font names, licensing/substitution needs, factual questions and verification limits. Distinguish screen-ready from printer-ready. Never call a flattened image a layered PSD or other native editable document.

Summarize the design decision and key changes briefly. Offer specific iteration axes—crop, title scale, information density, color balance—rather than an invitation to add decoration. Keep originals intact.

## Adapt design guidance, not frontend machinery

Transfer brief-specific direction, intentional type, real assets, compact tokens, a memorable gesture, restraint, truthful copy, and visual inspection.

Do not import navigation, fake buttons, equal feature-card grids, hover states, scroll reveals, mandatory animation, section-per-screen layouts, photo quotas, shaders or random palette selection. Do not make universal bans on fonts, colors, capitals, captions or festive illustration. Never apply website-demo rules that omit address numbers, fabricate contacts, or permit plausible invented prices to a real event flyer.
